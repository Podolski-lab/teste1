# PRD Quality Review — prd-teste1-2026-09-17 (Assistente de Produtividade Pessoal com Alexa)

## Overall verdict
This is a well-calibrated hobby PRD: it has a genuine, specific thesis (the gap is external pressure, not planning clarity — grounded in a year-stalled business project), FRs and Success Metrics that trace back to that thesis, and an honest Non-Goals/v2 list that distinguishes items from the original brief from items surfaced during this PRD's own discovery (via `addendum.md`). The risks are practical rather than structural: a couple of FRs (FR-1's timing, FR-2/FR-6's tone) implicitly promise more precision or judgment than the PRD's own stated constraints (NFR-2's calendar-sync limits, no tone rubric) can actually deliver, and a few real assumptions (calendar events always have an end time, tasks never overlap, the Alexa device is always reachable) are baked into the FRs without being flagged. None of this blocks the sole user/PM from building against the document, but tightening these points removes ambiguity before implementation.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
The document reads as decided, not as a menu — FR-11's manual-config choice, the single-retry checkpoint policy (FR-4), and the "silence during execution" stance (addendum) are all stated as settled calls, which fits a solo PRD where the user already made the calls during discovery. What's missing is any acknowledgment of what those calls cost. The addendum records "Decisão: nenhuma entra na v1" for the three mid-task-support candidates but never names the trade-off — that v1 leaves the entire execution window without any engagement mechanism at all, which is arguably the riskiest part of the loop for someone whose stated problem is *failure to execute*, not failure to plan.

### Findings
- **medium** Silent-execution trade-off not named (addendum.md, "Candidatos a mecanismo de apoio…") — The decision to defer all mid-task support to v2 is recorded, but the cost (zero support during the exact window where past behavior shows failure — a year-stalled project) is never stated. *Fix:* add one sentence in the addendum or PRD naming what's given up, even if the call stands.
- **medium** No Open Questions / `[NOTE FOR PM]` anywhere in the PRD — plausible for a hobby PRD where discovery resolved most things, but a few real edges remain unresolved without being marked as such (see Scope honesty findings below on unmarked assumptions). *Fix:* either state explicitly that discovery closed all open items, or add a short Open Questions/NOTE FOR PM block for the ones that remain (overlapping tasks, device unavailability).

## Substance over theater — strong
No personas section, no differentiation section, no boilerplate NFRs — appropriate omissions for this shape, not gaps. NFR-3's tone requirement ("parceiro compreensivo… nunca punitivo") could have been generic NFR theater, but it's earned: UJ-1 gives a concrete example script, FR-6 operationalizes it into the reprogramming flow, and the Success Metrics section explicitly names a counter-metric tied to the same risk ("se o uso virar fardo… isso é sinal de falha mesmo que a métrica de frequência de uso esteja batendo"). No findings.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is stated plainly in Overview ("o problema não é falta de clareza sobre o que fazer, é falta de pressão externa para executar") and is backed by a concrete instance (the year-stalled business project), not an abstract claim. Every FR traces to it: FR-2 (reconnect to purpose, not just name the task), FR-6 (non-punitive reprogramming, not punishment), FR-9 (per-pillar balance visible). The Success Metrics validate the thesis directly rather than measuring generic activity — "Movimento no pilar mais travado" is explicitly the same stalled project named in the Overview, and the "Contra-métrica" guards against the mechanism becoming self-defeating. No findings.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs carry a testable consequence (FR-3 through FR-5, FR-7 through FR-9, FR-11 are all checkable as written). Two categories fall short.

### Findings
- **high** FR-1's timing promise is in tension with NFR-2's own caveat (§ Requisitos Funcionais "FR-1" vs § Requisitos Não-Funcionais "NFR-2") — FR-1 says the reminder fires "no horário de início" of the scheduled task, but NFR-2 states the underlying Alexa↔Google Calendar integration lacks "sincronização instantânea." The PRD never reconciles what "on time" means given a sync limitation it has itself documented (and which the brief's addendum traces to real API constraints). A reader can't tell if FR-1 is a promise the integration can't keep. *Fix:* add an acceptable delay/tolerance to FR-1, or note explicitly that reminder timing is bounded by calendar poll/sync frequency (to be pinned at the architecture stage).
- **medium** FR-2 and FR-6 rest on a qualitative, unrubric'd judgment — "reconectar a tarefa ao propósito maior, não apenas nomear a tarefa" (FR-2) and "tom compreensivo (nunca punitivo)" (FR-6, also NFR-3) have exactly one worked example (UJ-1's script) and no other criterion for pass/fail. For a solo project this is lower-stakes (the user is also the reviewer), but it's still the kind of requirement downstream work would need a rubric for. *Fix:* 2-3 short example phrases (acceptable/not) or a minimal checklist ("names the pillar's purpose in ≤1 clause," "no 'você prometeu e não fez' framing").
- **low** FR-7/FR-8's time windows ("sexta-feira à tarde," "domingo, no final da tarde/noite") aren't bounded to hours, which the Lambda scheduler will eventually need. *Fix:* pin approximate ranges (e.g., "tarde = 14h–18h").

## Scope honesty — adequate
The "Fora do Escopo v1 / Candidatos v2" section does real work — it separates items carried over from the brief from items that surfaced during this PRD's own discovery, with an explicit addendum pointer, rather than silently dropping them. What's missing is any `[ASSUMPTION]` tagging for the inferences baked directly into the FRs.

### Findings
- **medium** Unmarked assumptions embedded in FR-3, FR-7/FR-8 (§ Requisitos Funcionais) — the PRD assumes without flagging it that (a) every relevant Google Calendar event has an explicit end time (FR-3's checkpoint fires "ao final da janela de tempo programada" — undefined for all-day or open-ended entries), (b) only one task is ever active at a time (no behavior specified for overlapping calendar events), and (c) the Alexa device is reachable whenever a reminder/checkpoint is due (no fallback for "not home"/device off, distinct from the addressed "no verbal response" case in FR-4). *Fix:* add `[ASSUMPTION: …]` tags on these three points (indexed at the end), or explicitly move "overlapping tasks" and "events without an end time" into Non-Goals if truly out of scope for v1.

## Downstream usability — adequate (matters less; likely standalone)
Nothing in the PRD indicates this feeds a separate architecture/story-creation pass beyond the solo author's own implementation, so this dimension carries less weight per the rubric. FR-1…FR-11, NFR-1…NFR-3, and UJ-1…UJ-2 are all contiguous, unique, and internally consistent — "pilar," "propósito maior," and "checkpoint" are used identically everywhere they appear.

### Findings
- **low** Success Metrics are the only major section without IDs (no SM-1…SM-5), unlike FRs/NFRs/UJs. Low stakes for a two-page standalone PRD, but would matter if this PRD is later cross-referenced (e.g., by an architecture or retrospective doc). *Fix:* number them if/when this PRD is consumed downstream.

## Shape fit — strong
This is a solo/hobby PRD and reads like one without being under-formalized: no persona theater, exactly two UJs matching the two real interaction loops (daily loop, weekly summary) rather than padding, a Non-Goals list doing real work, and Success Metrics that are operational/personal rather than market-shaped. Nothing here reads as forced into an enterprise template, and nothing load-bearing (accountability tone, purpose reconnection, pillar balance) is missing for the sake of brevity. No findings.
